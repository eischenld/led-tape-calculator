12V vs 24V LED Tape: Which Voltage Should You Use?

Compare 12V and 24V LED tape for current draw, voltage drop, maximum run length, cut intervals, power supplies, and real-world installation.

Quick answer: For most medium and long constant-voltage installations, 24V is the easier choice because it carries the same wattage at half the current of 12V. Choose 12V when the product, controller, cut interval, or project specifically favors it.

The basic electrical difference

LED tape is normally sold as a constant-voltage load. If two tapes produce the same light and consume the same wattage, a 24V version needs roughly half the current of a 12V version.

Current (A) = Power (W) ÷ Voltage (V)

A 120W load draws about 10A at 12V but only 5A at 24V. Lower current generally makes feeder-wire voltage drop easier to manage and reduces the current that connectors, dimmers, decoders, and distribution hardware must carry.

Why 24V usually wins on longer projects

Voltage drop is driven by current and resistance. Because 24V tape needs less current for a given wattage, the same feeder conductor produces less voltage drop in volts. That gives you more flexibility with power-supply location and branch length.

This does not mean a 24V strip can run forever from one end. The copper traces inside the tape have resistance too. Long sections can still lose brightness or shift color, especially when RGB or RGBW tape is driven at high output. Manufacturer maximum-run guidance and real measurements still matter.

When 12V makes sense

  • Short cut intervals matter. Many 12V products use smaller series groups than comparable 24V products, so cut points may be closer together.
  • The project already has a 12V source. Vehicles, batteries, control cabinets, and existing installations may make 12V the practical choice.
  • Your controller is designed around 12V. The tape, decoder, dimmer, and power supply must all be compatible with the same system voltage.
  • The exact tape you want is only available in 12V. LED density, CRI, color temperature, optics, and quality can matter more than voltage alone.

Do not mix the voltage accidentally

A 12V strip should not be connected to a 24V supply. A 24V strip connected to 12V will normally be very dim or fail to operate correctly. Treat the printed tape markings and manufacturer data as authoritative.

One more distinction: addressable/IC tape is a different decision. Common pixel products may be 5V, 12V, 24V, or internally regulated. Choose based on the exact pixel protocol and product datasheet rather than assuming the analog-tape rules apply directly.
